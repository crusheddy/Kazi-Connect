Where we work

Four sectors, deep focus

We deliberately concentrate on four sectors rather than claiming to serve everyone. Each one hires differently - different verification standards, different deadlines, different reasons a good candidate says no. These are the four our recruiters and HR Partners are built around, and the four our sourcing channels feed.

Sector 01

Banking & Financial Services

What makes hiring hard here

Branch expansion creates a wave of simultaneous vacancies with a fixed opening date - the branch opens whether or not the tellers are hired. Roles are compliance-sensitive, so a candidate who looks strong on paper can still fail verification late in the process, when there is no time left to restart.

Retention through probation matters as much as the placement itself. A hire who leaves in month two costs more than the vacancy did.

How we approach it

We run branch expansions as a single structured campaign rather than a series of separate roles, so sourcing effort is shared across positions and the opening date drives the plan backwards.

  • Credential and reference verification completed before shortlist, not after offer
  • Campaign timeline built backwards from the branch opening date
  • Probation tracked and reported, not assumed
  • Typical roles: branch staff, tellers, relationship officers, operations and support

Sector 02

NGOs & International Development

What makes hiring hard here

Programme roles are tied to donor funding cycles, which means a real, immovable deadline - if the team isn't in place, the grant conditions are at risk. Recruitment also has to be documented to a standard donors will accept on audit, which informal hiring cannot produce after the fact.

Roles are often specialised and fixed-term, which narrows the candidate pool considerably.

How we approach it

We work backwards from the donor deadline and keep the paper trail as we go, so the documentation exists when someone asks for it rather than being reconstructed.

  • Recruitment documented to a standard that survives donor audit
  • Campaign scheduled against the funding deadline from day one
  • Honest early warning when a role is unlikely to fill in the window
  • Typical roles: programme officers, M&E, finance and grants, field coordinators

Sector 03

Manufacturing & Private Sector

What makes hiring hard here

Skilled trades are rarely found through job boards - the best people are already working and are reached through trade networks and vocational institutions, not adverts. Plants also tend to grow past the point where informal HR works: fifty staff on handshake arrangements becomes a serious liability at a hundred and twenty.

Supervisory roles are the pinch point, needing both technical credibility on the floor and the ability to manage people.

How we approach it

Sourcing runs through VETA partnerships and trade associations rather than adverts alone. Where a company has outgrown informal HR, the Enterprise tier formalizes contracts and records alongside the hiring.

  • VETA and trade association sourcing channels
  • Practical assessment for skilled trades, not just interviews
  • HR formalization run in parallel with recruitment where needed
  • Typical roles: skilled trades, machine operators, supervisors, plant administration

Sector 04

Education

What makes hiring hard here

The academic year is a hard deadline that does not move, and academic appointments carry credential requirements that must withstand scrutiny from accreditation bodies. A qualification that cannot be verified with the issuing institution will be rejected - sometimes after the candidate has already been offered the post.

Senior academic hiring also runs on committee timelines, which need coordinating rather than chasing.

How we approach it

Verification is done early and directly with issuing institutions, including qualifications obtained abroad. Committee scheduling is treated as part of the job rather than the client's problem.

  • Credentials verified with issuing institutions before shortlist
  • Campaign timed against the start of the academic year
  • Appointment committee coordination handled by us
  • Typical roles: lecturers, academic administration, support and technical staff

Straight answer

What if you're not in one of these four?

Tell us anyway. If a role sits outside our sector focus and we don't think we'd do a good job on it, we'll say so rather than take the retainer - a badly filled role costs you more than an unfilled one. Where the software is the better answer, we'll point you to Kazi Connect HMS instead.
